Governments and Politics of Europe: interactive tools

Practice tools for PLSC 200N. Each one works through a historical case, a political science model, or an example of how an institution works, alongside that week's readings and lecture. They run in any current web browser, on a computer or a phone. There is nothing to install, no login, and nothing you do in a tool is recorded.

Democracy's collapse in interwar Germany (week 1)

  • Forming a government in the Weimar Reichstag

    Try to build a cabinet with a majority in the German parliament after each of four elections, 1928 to 1932, and watch the possible governments disappear.

  • The national Nazi vote, and who voted differently from it

    Six elections, 1924 to 1933: find the social groups that gave the Nazis more of their vote than the country as a whole did, and the groups that gave less.

Communist parties in postwar Eastern Europe (week 3)

  • Choosing your ministries

    Lead a Communist party joining a coalition cabinet in Hungary in 1945 or Czechoslovakia in 1946. Choose which ministries to ask for, then see where those choices leave you three years later.

Collective action and protest (week 4)

  • Would you take part?

    Work out when it makes sense for one person to join a protest, using the payoff table from lecture, then practice on problems like the ones in the homework.

Transitions to democracy (week 5)

  • The tipping model: why nobody saw 1989 coming

    Ten people each decide whether to oppose the government in public, depending on how many others already do. Make one person slightly more willing and see whether nothing happens or almost everyone joins in, then practice working out where a society ends up.

Electoral systems (week 9)

  • From votes to seats

    Turn votes into seats the way the lecture does for party list proportional representation: find the quota, give each party the seats its votes fully pay for, then hand the seats left over to the largest remainders. Step through a district one row at a time, then fill in the table yourself for districts you have not seen.

Week numbers refer to the Fall 2026 syllabus. Tools are added as the course reaches them.
